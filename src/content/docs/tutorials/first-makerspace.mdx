In [the first tutorial](/tutorials/first-install/) you installed Space Works and the installer created
a makerspace for you. Now you'll make it yours: give it its full name and public details, invite a
staff member, add your first item, print its QR label and check what the public sees.

Everything here happens in the browser, in the **staff console** at `/admin`. You only need the
terminal for one optional step.

| Thing | In this tutorial |
| --- | --- |
| Makerspace | **Kochi Makers Lab**, slug `kochi-makers` |
| Website | `https://makers.example.org` |
| Staff member | **Asha**, `asha@example.org`, who will be an **Inventory Manager** |
| First item | **Arduino Uno**, 5 in stock, in the **Electronics** category |

## What you'll build

- A makerspace called **Kochi Makers Lab** with its location, hours and currency filled in.
- Asha's staff account, able to run the inventory but not the makerspace's settings.
- An **Electronics** category holding an **Arduino Uno** product with 5 units.
- A printable QR label for the Arduino Uno.
- A public catalogue at `https://makers.example.org/m/kochi-makers` showing the Arduino Uno as
  **Available**, without revealing exactly how many you have.

## What you need

- A finished [first install](/tutorials/first-install/), and the superadmin username and password it
  gave you.
- Asha's email address, and a way to send her a link (email, chat or a message).
- A printer, if you want to stick the QR label on something today.

## Time

About 30 minutes.

## Words you'll meet

| Word | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Staff console** | The admin side of Space Works at `/admin`, where staff run the makerspace. The public sees the catalogue instead. |
| **Slug** | The short web-safe name in an address. `kochi-makers` is the slug in `/m/kochi-makers`. It is fixed when the makerspace is created. |
| **Superadmin** | You: the account that runs the whole server and can change everything, in every makerspace. |
| **Role** | A named set of permissions inside one makerspace, such as Inventory Manager. |
| **Module** | A whole feature area, such as events or machines, that can be switched on or off. |
| **Product** | One kind of thing you lend, like "Arduino Uno", with a stock count. |
| **QR label** | A printed square code. Scanning it tells Space Works exactly which item or box is in your hand. |

## Part 1: name and public details

<Steps>

1. **Sign in.** Open `https://makers.example.org/admin` and sign in as `admin` (with your
   authenticator code if you set one up). If the console asks you to choose a makerspace, pick
   **Kochi Makers**.

   **You should see** the **Dashboard**, with a setup checklist listing items such as
   **Set the profile and location**, **Add your first item** and **Invite staff**. Each one ticks
   itself off when you've done the work, so watch it fill up as you go.

2. **Give it its full name.** Open **Admin → Settings**. The page is titled **Makerspace settings**
   and is one long page of sections. In the first section, **Branding**:

   - Change **Registered name** from `Kochi Makers` to `Kochi Makers Lab`.
   - Choose **Save registered name**.

   The slug stays `kochi-makers`, so your public address doesn't change. That's why the first
   tutorial typed the shorter name into the installer.

   **Public display name** is optional. Fill it in only if you want visitors to see something
   different from the registered name; leave it blank to use the registered name.

3. **Add a logo and cover (optional).** Still in **Branding**, upload a **Logo** and a
   **Cover image (wide banner)**. Without a logo, the public site shows the name as text, which is
   fine to start with.

4. **Set the location.** Scroll to the **Location** section:

   - **Location label:** `Kochi, Kerala`
   - **Google Maps link:** optional. In Google Maps, use Share → Copy link and paste it here.

   Choose **Save location**. This label is shown on your public pages.

5. **Set hours and currency.** Scroll to **Location & hours**:

   - **Timezone:** check it says `Asia/Kolkata` (you chose it in the installer).
   - **Country:** India. Country is only visible to staff.
   - **Default currency:** check it says `inr`.
   - Fill in the weekly opening hours.

   Choose **Save location & hours**.

   **You should see** `Saved.` under the form.

</Steps>

## Part 2: check what's public and what's switched on

<Steps>

1. **Check the public switches.** In the **General** section of the same Settings page:

   - **Show public inventory** should be ticked (its badge says **On**). This is the catalogue
     visitors browse.
   - **Publish public site** should be ticked. It controls whether the makerspace appears in the
     public directory at all.
   - **Public home** can stay on **Automatic**. With lending switched on, `/m/kochi-makers` opens on
     the catalogue.

   Both switches are on for a new makerspace. Turning either off hides things but deletes nothing.

2. **Review the modules.** Open **Admin → Modules**. Modules are grouped by area. Each group shows a
   badge such as "3 of 5 installed", or **Always on** for the parts every makerspace has.

   Open a group to see each module, what it does and an **Install** or **Uninstall** button.
   The areas you chose in the installer (Inventory & lending, Machines and Reports) are already
   installed, so you don't need to change anything for this tutorial. Uninstalling a module later
   hides its screens and keeps its data.

   Only a superadmin sees this page. The installer's area menu is also available from the server:

   ```bash title="On the server, in /opt/spaceworks"
   bash scripts/update.sh --modules-only
   ```

   [Modules](/guides/modules/) explains each one.

</Steps>

<Aside type="tip">
Smaller switches inside a module, such as loan renewals or kiosk stations, live in the
**Feature settings** section of **Admin → Settings**.
</Aside>

## Part 3: invite Asha as Inventory Manager

An **Inventory Manager** can run the whole lending side: add and edit inventory, print QR labels,
review borrow requests, hand items out and take them back. They can't change the makerspace's
settings, staff or money. That suits Asha, who looks after the lab's tools.

<Steps>

1. **Create the invitation.** Open **Admin → Users** and scroll to **Staff invitations**:

   - **Email:** `asha@example.org`
   - **Role:** Inventory Manager

   Choose **Invite staff**.

   **You should see** a delivery status, the words `You can also share this link privately:` and a
   box holding the invitation link. The invitation also appears in the list below with its expiry
   date. Invitations last 7 days.

2. **Get the link to Asha.** If you've set up [email](/configure/email/), Asha receives the link by
   email. If not (a brand-new install has no email yet), click into the **Invitation link** box, copy
   the link and send it to Asha yourself. Treat it like a password: anyone with the link can accept
   it before Asha does.

   Lost it? Use **Resend** beside the invitation to get a fresh link, or **Revoke** to cancel it.

3. **Asha accepts.** Asha opens the link. It goes to `https://makers.example.org/staff-invitations/accept`.

   **She should see** the heading **Accept your staff invitation**. She chooses a strong
   **Password** for her new account and selects **Accept invitation**.

4. **Asha signs in.** Asha goes to `https://makers.example.org/admin` and signs in with her **email
   address** as the username, plus the password she just chose.

   **She should see** the console with the **Inventory** menu group, including **Inventory**,
   **Categories** and **QR Tools**, but no **Settings** or **Users**.

</Steps>

<Aside type="note">
Prefer to hand out a username and password yourself, for example for a shared front-desk login? Use
**Add staff** on the same Users page instead. See
[Roles and permissions](/guides/roles-and-permissions/) for every role and how to build narrower ones.
</Aside>

## Part 4: add a category and your first item

You can do this part as yourself or as Asha.

<Steps>

1. **Add the Electronics category.** Open **Inventory → Categories**. In the form at the top:

   - **Name:** `Electronics`
   - **Slug:** leave empty. Its placeholder says "auto from name", so it becomes `electronics`.
   - **Order** and **Icon:** optional.

   Choose **Add category**.

   **You should see** a row for **Electronics** in the table, with `0` in the **# products** column.

2. **Add the Arduino Uno.** Open **Inventory → Inventory** and choose **Add item**. Fill in:

   | Field | Value | Why |
   | --- | --- | --- |
   | **Name** | `Arduino Uno` | What people search for. |
   | **Tracking mode** | Quantity | You count how many you have, rather than tracking each board separately. |
   | **Category** | Electronics | Groups it on the public catalogue. |
   | **Description** | `Microcontroller board with USB cable.` | Shown to borrowers. |
   | **Public borrowing terms** | `Return with the USB cable.` | Optional rules shown on the item's public page. |
   | **Storage location** | `Shelf B3` | Staff only. Never shown to the public. |
   | **Total quantity** | `5` | How many you own. |
   | **Available quantity** | `5` | How many are on the shelf right now. Both start at `1`, so change both. |
   | **Public** | Ticked | Lists the item on the public catalogue. |
   | **Self checkout** | Unticked | Keep loans staff-reviewed for now. |
   | **Show count** | Unticked | Don't show the exact number publicly. |
   | **Public visibility** | Status only | Show a simple label instead of a number. |

   The other product details (manufacturer, model, barcode and so on) are optional. Choose **Save**.

   **You should see** the item open again for editing, so you can upload a photo, and an
   **Arduino Uno** row in the inventory list with a total of 5 and 5 available. Back in
   **Categories**, Electronics now shows `1` product.

</Steps>

### What "Status only" shows the public

You picked **Status only**, so visitors see a word instead of a number:

| Label | When |
| --- | --- |
| **Available** | More than 20% of the total is on the shelf |
| **Limited** | 20% of the total or less is available (rounded up, so 1 of 5) |
| **Unavailable** | Nothing is available |

With 5 of 5 on the shelf, the Arduino Uno shows **Available**. When only one is left it switches
to **Limited**, all by itself. To show the exact number instead, choose **Exact count** and also
tick **Show count**; the number appears only when both are set. More in
[Inventory and QR codes](/guides/inventory-and-qr/).

## Part 5: print a QR label

A QR label lets staff scan the item instead of searching for it, and every scan is recorded.

<Steps>

1. **Start a print batch.** Open **Inventory → QR Tools** and choose **New batch**. In
   **Create QR print batch**, set **Batch title** to `Electronics shelf` and choose **Create batch**.

   The new batch is selected for you in **Print batch**.

2. **Add the Arduino Uno's label.** Under **Inventory QR**, choose the **Inventory item**.

   **You should see** the option `Arduino Uno | quantity | 5 available`.

   Because the Arduino Uno is tracked by quantity, it gets one label for the product. Choose
   **Add/reprint item QR**.

   **You should see** the label appear in the batch, and the batch says `1 QR labels accumulated`.

3. **Download and print.** Choose **Download all (ZIP)**. The ZIP file contains one SVG image per
   label. Open the image in your browser or any image viewer, print it, and stick it on the Arduino
   Uno storage bin.

</Steps>

<Aside type="note">
Want a separate label on every board, so you know exactly which one went out? Use **Individual**
tracking instead of Quantity. QR Tools then generates one label per unit. Separately, staff hand every
reviewed loan over in a labelled **box**. Both are covered in
[Inventory and QR codes](/guides/inventory-and-qr/).
</Aside>

## Part 6: see it as the public does

<Steps>

1. **Open a private window.** Use your browser's private or incognito mode, so you see the site as a
   visitor who isn't signed in.

2. **Go to the catalogue.** Open `https://makers.example.org/m/kochi-makers`.

   You can also just open `https://makers.example.org`: while Kochi Makers Lab is the only public
   makerspace on the server, the home page sends visitors straight to it.

   **You should see** the name **Kochi Makers Lab**, a search box that says
   "Search tools, machines, kits, or materials", an **Electronics** category, and **Arduino Uno**
   marked **Available**.

3. **Check what's hidden.** The public page doesn't show **Shelf B3**, the QR code or the exact
   count. Storage locations, QR codes, box IDs and scan history are never shown publicly.

</Steps>

Your makerspace is ready for its first loan.

## If something went wrong

- **The Arduino Uno isn't on the public page.** Check that **Public** is ticked on the item, and that
  **Show public inventory** and **Publish public site** are both ticked in **Admin → Settings**.
  Archived items never appear.
- **The public page says `No public programme is available.`** The public site is switched off, or
  no public area (such as lending) is installed. Check **Publish public site** and
  **Admin → Modules**.
- **The page says `Unable to load this makerspace.`** Check the slug in the address. It's
  `kochi-makers`, from the name you typed in the installer, not `kochi-makers-lab`.
- **Asha's invitation email never arrived.** A new install has no email set up. Copy the invitation
  link from **Staff invitations** and send it yourself, or set up [email](/configure/email/) and choose
  **Resend**.
- **There's no QR Tools menu item.** It needs the QR print batches module, part of the Inventory &
  lending area, and the `manage_qr` permission. Check **Admin → Modules** and the person's role.

More fixes are in [Troubleshooting](/help/troubleshooting/).
