In this tutorial you run one complete loan through Space Works. A member asks to borrow a tool, a staff
member reviews the request, hands the tool over with evidence, and later takes it back. Along the way
you will see exactly how the stock numbers move and where every action is recorded.

The running example:

| Who or what | In this tutorial |
| --- | --- |
| Makerspace | **Kochi Makers Lab**, slug `kochi-makers`, at `https://makers.example.org` |
| Staff member | **Asha**, who holds the **Inventory Manager** role |
| Member | **Ravi**, who wants to borrow something |
| Product | **Arduino Uno**, 5 in stock |

## What you'll build

One finished loan: Ravi's request for 1 Arduino Uno goes from **pending review** to **accepted**,
**issued** and finally **returned**, with a box scan, an issue photo, a return photo and a return
remark attached. You will also try the two faster routes, QR self-checkout and direct handout.

## What you need

- A running Space Works with the makerspace from [Tutorial: your first makerspace](/tutorials/first-makerspace/):
  Kochi Makers Lab, the **Arduino Uno** product (total quantity 5, marked **Public**) and Asha's staff
  account with the Inventory Manager role.
- At least one **box** with a QR label stuck on it. Every reviewed loan goes out in a box. See
  [Boxes and containers](/guides/inventory-and-qr/#boxes-and-containers) if you do not have one yet.
- Working email, so Ravi can verify his new account. See [Email](/configure/email/).
- A phone or laptop with a camera, or any JPEG, PNG or WebP photo to use as evidence.

## Time

About 20 minutes.

## The idea in one picture

```
Ravi asks ──► Asha accepts ──► Asha issues ──► Ravi uses it ──► Asha takes it back
 pending        accepted          issued                           returned
              (stock reserved)  (box scan +                     (box scan + return
                                 issue photo)                    photo + remark)
```

Space Works keeps several counters for every product. These are the ones that move in this tutorial:

| Counter | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Total** | How many you own |
| **Available** | On the shelf and free to lend |
| **Reserved** | Promised to an accepted request, but not handed over yet |
| **Issued** | Out with a borrower right now |

You never edit **Available** by hand during a loan. The loan workflow moves units between these
counters for you, so the numbers always add up.

<Aside type="note" title="Reserved has no column of its own">
The staff inventory table shows **Total**, **Available**, **Issued**, **Damaged** and **Lost**.
Reserved units are the ones missing from that row: Total minus Available, Issued, Damaged and Lost.
</Aside>

## Before you start: note the starting numbers

<Steps>

1. Sign in to the staff console at `https://makers.example.org/admin/` as Asha.

2. Open **Inventory → Inventory** and find **Arduino Uno**.

   **You should see:** **Total** 5, **Available** 5, **Issued** 0.

3. In another browser (or a private window), open the public catalogue at
   `https://makers.example.org/m/kochi-makers`.

   **You should see:** the Arduino Uno card with an **Available** badge. If Asha set the product's
   **Public visibility** to **Exact count** and turned on **Show count**, the badge reads
   **Available (5)** instead.

</Steps>

## Part 1: Ravi creates an account and asks to borrow

<Steps>

1. In the private window, open `https://makers.example.org/m/kochi-makers/member`.

   **You should see:** a **Member sign in** form.

2. Choose **Create a member account**. Fill in **Name** (`Ravi`), **Email** (`ravi@example.org`) and
   **Password**. **Phone** is optional. Choose **Create account**.

   **You should see:** "Check your email to verify the new account, then sign in."

3. Sign in with the same email and password. Space Works asks for the code it emailed to Ravi.
   Type it into **Verification code** and choose **Verify email**.

   Until the email is verified, the account can do nothing except verify itself. This is how
   Space Works knows the requester's contact details are real.

4. Go back to the catalogue at `https://makers.example.org/m/kochi-makers`.

5. On the **Arduino Uno** card, press **+** once. The counter between **-** and **+** shows `1`.

   **You should see:** a **Borrow request** panel listing **Arduino Uno x1** under
   **Selected equipment**.

6. Under **Request purpose**, write why Ravi needs it, for example
   `Building a soil moisture sensor for the community garden`.

7. Choose **Submit request**.

   **You should see:** **Request submitted** in a green box.

</Steps>

**Stock after this step:** nothing changes yet. A request is only a question; no unit is set aside
until staff accept it.

| Total | Available | Reserved | Issued |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5 | 5 | 0 | 0 |

**Audit log:** a `request.submitted` entry, made by Ravi.

<Aside type="note" title="Who can submit a request?">
This tutorial assumes the default setup, where the **Membership** module is off and anyone who is
signed in can ask to borrow. If your makerspace installed the Membership module, Ravi must also be an
active member who has accepted the waiver and is checked in. See
[Loans](/guides/loans/#1-a-member-submits-a-request).
</Aside>

## Part 2: Asha reviews and accepts

<Steps>

1. In Asha's staff console, open **Operate → Requests**.

   **You should see:** Ravi's request in the **Pending review** panel, with his purpose text.

2. Choose **Accept** on that request.

   **You should see:** a dialog titled **Accept request #7** (your number will differ), with the line
   **Arduino Uno (requested 1)** and a quantity box set to `1`.

   You could lower the quantity to accept only part of a request, or set it to `0` to decline one
   item. At least one unit must be accepted; otherwise use **Reject**, which asks for a reason.

3. Leave the quantity at `1` and choose **Accept**.

   **You should see:** the request move from **Pending review** to **Ready for handover**.

4. Open **Inventory → Inventory** again and look at the Arduino Uno row.

   **You should see:** **Total** 5, **Available** 4, **Issued** 0.

</Steps>

**Stock after this step:** accepting **reserves** the unit. It is still on the shelf, but nobody else
can be promised it.

| Total | Available | Reserved | Issued |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5 | 4 | 1 | 0 |

On the public catalogue the badge still reads **Available**, or **Available (4)** with exact counts on.
The status label only drops to **Limited** when 20% or less of the total is available (here, 1 unit or
fewer).

**Audit log:** a `request.accepted` entry, made by Asha, which records the accepted quantity.

## Part 3: Asha issues the Arduino

Issuing is the physical handover, and it is where Space Works insists on evidence. Two things are
required, and the request cannot be issued without both:

- **A box QR scan.** The Arduino goes out in a box, and Asha scans the QR label on that box. This ties
  the loan to one physical container, so the scan history shows where it went, and on return the
  same box must be scanned again. The box is a shared, checkable fact: "this kit left in box B-12".
- **An issue photo.** Asha photographs what she is handing over. If the Arduino later comes back
  with a bent header pin, the issue photo shows what condition it left in. Photos go to private
  storage and are only ever shown to staff through short-lived links; they never appear on the public
  site.

Together with the audit log, these turn "I think Ravi had it" into evidence anyone on the team can
check later.

<Steps>

1. In **Operate → Requests**, find Ravi's request under **Ready for handover** and choose
   **Guided issue**.

   **You should see:** a dialog titled **Issue request #7** with "Step 1 of 5: Scan container".

2. Choose **Scan box QR** and point the camera at the box's QR label. If you have no camera, type the
   label's payload into **Box QR payload** and choose **Check box QR**.

   **You should see:** "Verified box:" followed by the box's code. Choose **Next**.

3. **Check borrower card.** The dialog shows **Borrower: Ravi**. Check Ravi's identity in person,
   tick **I checked the borrower against this request** and choose **Next**.

4. **Check items.** The dialog shows **Arduino Uno** with **Accepted quantity: 1**. Make sure the
   board in your hand matches, then choose **Next**.

5. **Photo and confirmation.** Use the file picker (or **Camera**) to add a photo of the Arduino.

   **You should see:** **Photo uploaded**, then "Uploaded photo #" and a number.

   Optionally add an **Issue remark**, such as `Board with USB cable`.

6. Choose **Confirm issue**.

   **You should see:** **Issue recorded**, with the request number, the evidence photo number, the box
   code and the line **Arduino Uno: 1 issued**. You can **Print receipt** for Ravi, then
   **Close receipt**.

7. Back in **Operate → Requests**, the request is now under **Active loans** with a due date, for
   example "Due" followed by a date 7 days from now (the default loan period).

8. Check **Inventory → Inventory** once more.

   **You should see:** **Total** 5, **Available** 4, **Issued** 1.

</Steps>

**Stock after this step:** the reserved unit becomes an issued unit.

| Total | Available | Reserved | Issued |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5 | 4 | 0 | 1 |

**Audit log:** `box.assigned` and `box.scanned` for the box, `evidence.attached` for the photo, and
`request.issued`, all made by Asha.

<Aside type="tip" title="If issuing is refused">
"Box is already out on another loan." means that box is still attached to an unreturned loan; use a
different box. "Issue evidence has not been uploaded." means the photo did not finish uploading; add
it again and wait for **Photo uploaded**.
</Aside>

## Part 4: Ravi uses the Arduino

Nothing happens in Space Works while Ravi builds his sensor. The loan simply sits in **Active loans**
until its due date. If your makerspace sends email and has a loan policy saved, Ravi gets a reminder
around the due time. Staff can change the date with **Set due** on the request.

## Part 5: Asha processes the return

A return also needs evidence: the **same box scanned again**, a **return photo** and a **return
remark**. The remark is required because it is where staff write down the condition, which matters
most when something comes back damaged.

<Steps>

1. When Ravi brings the box back, open **Operate → Requests**, find his loan under **Active loans**
   and choose **Guided return**.

   **You should see:** **Return request #7**, "Step 1 of 5: Scan container".

2. Scan the box's QR label (or type its payload and choose **Check box QR**), then **Next**.

   It must be the box the Arduino went out in. A different box is refused with "Scanned box does not
   match this loan."

3. Tick **I checked the borrower against this request** and choose **Next**.

4. **Check items.** The Arduino Uno shows **Remaining: 1**, with fields for **Returned**, **Damaged**
   and **Missing**. **Returned** is already `1`. If the board is fine, leave it and choose **Next**.

5. Add a return photo of the Arduino. To compare it with how the board left, use
   **View issue photo** on the request in **Active loans** (before or after this dialog).

6. In **Return remark (required)**, write what you found, for example
   `Returned in good condition, cable included`.

7. Choose **Confirm return**.

   **You should see:** **Return recorded** and the line **Arduino Uno: 1 returned, 0 damaged,
   0 missing**.

8. Check **Inventory → Inventory**.

   **You should see:** **Total** 5, **Available** 5, **Issued** 0. On the public catalogue the badge
   reads **Available**, or **Available (5)** with exact counts on.

</Steps>

**Stock after this step:** the unit is back on the shelf.

| Total | Available | Reserved | Issued |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5 | 5 | 0 | 0 |

**Audit log:** `request.returned`, `evidence.attached` for the return photo and `box.scanned`, all
made by Asha. The finished loan moves to the request **History** (choose **Show history** to see it).

If Asha had recorded the board as **Damaged** instead, it would go to the **Damaged** count rather
than back to **Available**, the request would end as **closed with issue**, and an accountability
record would be written against Ravi. See
[Damage, loss and accountability](/guides/loans/#damage-loss-and-accountability).

## See the whole story in the audit log

<Steps>

1. Open **Insights → Audit log**. Inventory Managers can see it because the role includes
   `view_audit`.

2. Leave the filters empty to see every event, newest first. To narrow it down, type one complete
   action code into the **Action** filter, for example `request.accepted`. The filter matches the
   whole code exactly, so a partial code such as `request.` finds nothing.

   **You should see:** each entry with a readable name (for example **Request accepted**) next to its
   action code (`request.accepted`), who did it and when.

3. For a single loan, go back to **Operate → Requests** and choose **View timeline** on the request.
   It shows that request's events in order.

</Steps>

The whole loan, in order:

| Stage | Action codes | Recorded for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Request | `request.submitted` | Ravi |
| Accept | `request.accepted` | Asha |
| Issue | `box.assigned`, `box.scanned`, `evidence.attached`, `request.issued` | Asha |
| Return | `request.returned`, `evidence.attached`, `box.scanned` | Asha |

Audit entries are append-only. They cannot be edited or deleted, even by a superadmin, which is what
makes them trustworthy months later.

## The same loan, two faster ways

The reviewed request is the safest route because a person approves every loan first. Space Works has
two quicker routes for tools you trust people with. Both skip the review, so units go straight from
**Available** to **Issued** with no reserved step, and both still require a photo.

<Tabs>
<TabItem label="QR self-checkout">

Ravi borrows the Arduino himself, with no staff at the counter. This needs the **Self checkout**
feature, the Arduino marked **Enabled for public self-checkout** with its own QR label, and Ravi must
be an active member who is checked in at the space.

1. Ravi opens `https://makers.example.org/m/kochi-makers/checkout` and stays on
   **Use (check out)**.
2. He uploads a photo of the Arduino.
3. He chooses **Scan QR** and scans the label on the Arduino.

Stock goes from 5 available to 4 available and 1 issued at once. The loan appears in Asha's
**Active loans** like any other, and the audit log shows `public_tool.checked_out`.

To return it, Ravi opens the same page, chooses **Return**, uploads a return photo, fills in
**Return condition notes** and scans the QR again. The audit log shows `public_tool.returned`.

</TabItem>
<TabItem label="Direct handout">

Asha lends the Arduino on the spot, with no request from Ravi. This needs the **Self checkout**
feature and the `issue_direct_loan` action, which Inventory Managers have.

:::caution[Ravi needs a membership and a check-in first]
A direct handout only goes to an active member who is at the space right now. Ravi must have an
active membership at Kochi Makers Lab, have accepted any waiver the space requires, and be checked in.
The setup in these tutorials gives Ravi an account but no membership, so he won't appear in the
**Borrower** list until you add one. See [Membership](/guides/membership/) and
[Kiosk](/guides/kiosk/) for adding members and checking them in.
:::

1. Asha opens **Operate → Direct handout** and chooses **Guided handout**.
2. **Borrower card:** she picks Ravi under **Borrower** (or scans his member card). The list shows
   active members.
3. **Scan or select tools:** she scans the Arduino's QR, or types `1` next to **Arduino Uno**.
4. **Issue photo:** she adds a photo and an optional **Issue remark**, then chooses
   **Confirm handout**.

Stock goes from 5 available to 4 available and 1 issued. The audit log shows
`admin_direct.checked_out`. The return is processed from the same tab, again with a return photo
and remark, and is logged as `admin_direct.returned`.

</TabItem>
</Tabs>

For the full rules behind each route, see [QR self-checkout](/guides/loans/#qr-self-checkout) and
[Direct handouts](/guides/loans/#direct-handouts).

## If something went wrong

- **Ravi's request fails with an authentication error.** He is not signed in, or his email is not
  verified yet. Sign in again from `/m/kochi-makers/member` and finish verification first.
- **There is no Guided issue button.** Asha's role needs `issue_request`, `assign_box` and
  `upload_evidence`. See [Roles and permissions](/guides/roles-and-permissions/).
- **The photo upload fails.** Object storage is not reachable from the browser. See
  [Object storage](/configure/object-storage/).
- **Direct handout or self-checkout is missing.** The **Self checkout** feature is off for the
  makerspace. See [Modules](/guides/modules/).

For anything else, see [Troubleshooting](/help/troubleshooting/).
